Hazardous Elements in Electronic Waste & Biological Impact

How improper disposal and informal burning threaten public health and ecosystems:

Chemical Element Common Electronic Source Human Health Effects Environmental & Soil Consequences
Lead (Pb) Circuit board solder, CRT screen funnels, lead-acid batteries Severe central and peripheral nervous system impairment, cognitive deficits in children, chronic kidney disease. Infiltrates groundwater; bioaccumulates in agricultural crops causing long-term soil toxicity.
Mercury (Hg) Cold-cathode fluorescent backlights, circuit relays, thermostats Minamata syndrome, acute neurotoxicity, respiratory damage upon inhaling vapor, sensory loss. Methylated by aquatic bacteria into methylmercury; accumulates exponentially up the aquatic food chain.
Cadmium (Cd) SMD chip resistors, Ni-Cd rechargeable batteries, semiconductors Itai-Itai disease, osteomalacia (severe bone demineralization), lung emphysema, renal cortical necrosis. Highly mobile in acidic soils; taken up by staple crops such as rice and wheat, entering human diet.
Brominated Flame Retardants (BFRs) Plastic computer casings, printed circuit boards Endocrine disruption, thyroid hormone interference, immunological suppression, potential reproductive harm. When incinerated in open air, converts into toxic polybrominated dioxins and furans that persist for decades.
Hexavalent Chromium (Cr VI) Galvanized metal chassis coatings, anti-corrosive treatments Asthmatic attacks, severe nasal septum perforations, DNA damage, pulmonary cancer. Strong oxidizing agent that easily leaches into local rivers and alters aquatic microbiological balance.

Resource Revolution

Urban Mining: Recovering Value, Saving the Planet

Electronics are modern high-grade mineral deposits. 1 tonne of smartphones contains approximately 300g of gold, 2kg of silver, and 130kg of copper. In contrast, 1 tonne of raw gold ore mined from the earth yields less than 5g of gold.

By channelizing e-waste through scientific hydro-metallurgical recyclers, society avoids opencast mining devastation, cuts chemical leaching pollution, and decreases carbon emissions by up to 80% per ounce of metal recovered.

Gold per Tonne (Smartphones): ~300 grams
Gold per Tonne (Mined Ore): < 5 grams
Carbon Footprint Reduction: 80% Less CO₂
Landfill Diversion: 100% Zero Landfill
